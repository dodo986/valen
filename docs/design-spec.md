# Valen — design spec

**Direction** — A dark page in one colour — the playing cover's, in Material-style tones — and floating chrome. The language comes from two apps Valen already builds on: Mochy Music (full-bleed hero that fades into the page, tight semi-bold type, superellipse covers, a floating capsule player and tab bar, the cover's colours as drifting light) and Orpheon's Immersive look (black page with white-alpha text steps, translucent bars with a light edge, white chips, cover-flush quick tiles, a stage of bare transport glyphs over the cover's tint). Valen's own part is the second voice in the type, the way the same pieces serve a desktop window, and two things neither app has: **liquid glass** chrome that bends the page at its rim, and a **street layer** drawn in one pen — the owner's own tag and the round marker it was written with. The app is a dark wall; the chrome is wet glass in front of it; the marks are paint on it. Motion is physical in the same three materials: glass pours and wobbles, paint is written, poured and drips, paper is slapped on and taped.

Not: neon (glows, halos, saturated lit edges), more than one hue at a time, stacked glass, a font standing in for the tag, graffiti on anything a person reads to use the app, decorative gradients unrelated to artwork, stock card grids, uppercase eyebrow labels, icons in circles as empty states, purple-blue gradients, emoji icons, placeholder copy, shadows.

**Type** — Two voices, one rule for which speaks.
- *The app* speaks in Bricolage Grotesque SemiBold: the greeting, screen titles, section titles, the wordmark. Scale: 60/60 (−0.035em) · 44/46 (−0.03em) · 34/36 (−0.03em) · 28/32 (−0.025em) · 22/26 (−0.02em) · 19/24 (−0.02em).
- *The music* is set in Inter: every song title, artist and control. Titles and labels are SemiBold, secondary lines Medium; nothing is Regular. Scale: 18/24 (−0.03em) · 15/20 (−0.025em) · 13/18 (−0.02em) semi-bold; 16/24 (−0.02em) · 14/20 (−0.015em) · 13/18 (−0.012em) medium; 11/14 medium for times (12/16 under the stage rail).
- Song titles as headlines (`TrackType`): 52/54 on a wide Home hero, 40/44 on the stage and the phone hero, 24/28 on the phone stage and beside the queue, all Inter SemiBold at −0.035 to −0.045em. Song titles are Inter, never Bricolage, because they arrive in any script and must not change typeface mid-title.
- Tracking tightens as size grows. **Nothing is uppercase.** Times and counts use tabular figures (`timeStyle`).
- A lead-in is two-tone, not a label above a title: "Playing from" at 62% white, then the service in full white.
- *The tag* is not a typeface. It is the owner's drawing (`docs/tag-source.png`), traced to the centre lines of its 18 marker strokes in writing order (`ui/components/TagStrokes.kt`) and drawn with a round nib, so the app writes its name rather than showing a picture of it. It signs the side panel (the V alone on the rail) and the phone's Home. No other hand-lettering exists in the app.
- Assets: static TTFs bundled at `ui/src/commonMain/composeResources/font/` — `bricolage_semibold.ttf`, `inter_regular.ttf`, `inter_medium.ttf`, `inter_semibold.ttf`. Both families OFL. No system font is requested.

**Space & shape** — 4pt grid: 4 8 12 16 20 24 32 40 48 64 96. Gutters: phone 20, rail 28, desktop 40. Row 64.
Shapes are superellipses (`Squircle`, exponent 5), given as *reach* along the edge, which reads tighter than a circular radius of the same number: covers in rows 12 · rows, quick tiles 18 · grouped cards, shelf covers 28 · stage cover, hero cover, side panel 40. Controls a finger presses (buttons, chips, the search field, the dock, the tab bar) are pills. One shape per component. A cover that fills the end of a tile has square corners and is rounded by the tile, so the two share one curve.

**Colour: one seed, in tones (dark only)** — Like Material 3's dynamic colour, the whole app is drawn from a single seed. **By default it is black and pink**: seed `#E84C8B`, the page true black and the surfaces neutral, so only marks, selection and glass tint carry the pink. Settings → Colour can switch to *From the cover*, where the seed is the playing cover's accent and the surfaces take a breath of its hue. The seed's hue is taken at fixed OKLCH lightness and gentle chroma (`theme/Tonal.kt`, `ValenScheme`), so the screen is always one soft family and never two hues, never a saturated or glowing edge. A grey cover gives a near-neutral scheme (chroma scaled down with the seed's own). The scheme eases to a new song's over 600ms. Roles (OKLCH L / C):
- *Page* .155/.012 · *surface* .215/.018 · *surface2* .265/.022 · *skeleton* .205/.014 — dark, with a breath of the hue.
- *Primary* .84/.095 (soft pastel) with *on primary* .27/.06 — the filled button, a chosen chip or segment, the active part of a rail, the tag, the marker lines, the paint of a pour, the playing bars.
- *Container* .37/.075 with *on container* .93/.04 — what is selected: the glass droplet, the tonal (second) button, the marker swipe behind the playing row.
- *Glass* veil .20/.016 at 62%, opaque fallback .22/.018 at 93%; *player tint* .30/.05, mixed 50% into the veil.
- *Wash* (`AmbientWash`): floor .19/.03 and three pools of the one hue at .40, .34 and .29 — top left, right, low centre — dithered, under a veil that fades toward the page. One wash per screen at most: behind the Home hero and greeting, behind a playlist's cover and name, and filling the stage, where it drifts slowly (96 s loop) while a song plays. With nothing playing, Home has no wash.
- Text is white at three alphas, 100% · 62% · 38%, so it reads on every tone. Separator 1dp at 10% white, only between rows of one group. Hover 6% white, press 10%. Scrim over a cover that carries a mark: black at 52%. Danger `#FF5A5F` and success `#3DD68C` are the only other hues, and only for state.
- The accent is measured as before: pixels in 18 hue bins weighted by saturation and brightness, under 6% coloured pixels gives grey, desaturated 15%. Only covers of the playing song are measured.

**Chrome: liquid glass** — The side panel, the player dock, the phone's mini player and tab bar, toasts and, on a wide window, the song menu are glass; nothing else is. Each piece draws the pages behind it: the pages are recorded once a frame into a layer (`Backdrop`, `Modifier.backdropSource`) and each glass shape (`GlassSurface`) draws its own piece of it blurred 30dp, saturated ×1.55, under the scheme's glass veil (mixed 50% with the player tint on the dock and mini player). Its rim bends what is behind it like the edge of a lens: inside a 22dp band, the page is sampled up to 14dp further out along the edge's normal, red and blue 14% further and nearer than green (`LensShader`, SkSL on the desktop, AGSL on Android 13+; Android 12 blurs without the rim; Android 10–11 falls back to the scheme's opaque glass fill). Light, kept quiet: a 1dp hairline at 12% white, a rim highlight brightest at the top (36% white) with a faint return at the bottom, a sheen over the top 120dp, and on a desktop a glint that follows the pointer round the rim. Chrome never draws chrome: the pages are the only source, so glass never sees glass. Inset 12dp from the window and from other chrome. Content scrolls under the bottom chrome and dims to black beneath it.
Where you are inside glass is a *droplet* of the scheme's container (78%), with its own rim light, and the place's icon and name in on-container. Its two ends move on different springs, the one reaching for the new place quick (900, .72) and the other slow (240, .78), so it stretches toward the new place and gathers itself there. In the side panel a fainter droplet follows the pointer between places. At its foot the panel carries the private mode switch — an eye with a line through it (Material's `VisibilityOff`, no service logo) and the app's `Toggle`, the glyph alone and tinted when on in the rail: on, nothing about what is listened to is counted. It is a thing flipped on the way past, not a place, so it sits outside the list.

**The wall and the street layer** — The page carries a fixed grain of light and dark specks (7% / 4%), like sprayed concrete; glass blurs it away. Street marks are drawn in one pen, the round marker of the tag: the tag (written stroke by stroke over 1.5s in the primary tone, flat paint with no halo — only a fine overspray of specks — and two drips; it rewrites when pressed; the overspray thickens under the pointer), a marker line under each page title and the greeting (drawn on after the letters land, then one drip), and the marker swipe behind the title of the row that is playing (the container tone). Covers that stand alone (the Home hero, the stage) are *pasted*: turned up to 1.6° by a seed from the song, straightened under the pointer, and held by two strips of warm tape (`#EDE4D3` at 44%) that slap on with each new song. Shelf and playlist covers are pasted without tape.

**Motion** (one file: `Motion.kt`) — standard 280ms `cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)`; enter 320ms `cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)`; exit 200ms `cubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1)`; artwork crossfade 320ms; sheet 320/240ms; player tint 600ms; wash to a new cover 1400ms.
- *Pages* arrive by place (`components/Arrivals.kt`): Home is poured, Search and Import are sprayed out in a spatter burst from the title, Playlists is rolled on left to right and Queue right to left, Settings comes up behind a shop shutter, the stage is slapped on like a poster, Profile switches on like a tube. The pour: the new page is revealed down the window behind a wavering edge with drips running ahead of it, a 4% band of wet paint in the primary tone leading, the new page painted solid behind (620ms, `cubic-bezier(.55,0,.18,1)`); the old page sinks to 96.5% and 35% under it. Then the page's items arrive 38ms apart — rows rise 18dp, covers and cards slap down from 125% and a few degrees, section titles swing in from the left — the first ten only, and only within 650ms of the page appearing (a scroll never replays it).
- *Type*: a screen title, the greeting and a song title on the hero and the stage rise letter by letter through their line (520ms each, 22ms apart, back-out `cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)`), drawn as one paragraph clipped per letter.
- *Glass*: the player is born as a drop its own height across and blooms into the capsule (spring 170, .7); a new song makes it gulp (jelly spring 300, .32: 3% wider, 10% shorter, ringing out) while a band of light crosses it (1200ms); while a song plays, two points of light run round its rim (6.4s a turn).
- *Paint*: the tag at a marker's pace (1.5s, the V alone in .75s), drips 900ms; the marker line 720ms.
- *Press* squeezes to 94% and springs back with overshoot (700, .5). Covers lean up to 7° toward the pointer (260, .72); a hovered card comes up 3.5%; the stage cover sits back to 90% while paused; play and pause turn into each other (quarter turn, scale from 40%, 260ms).
- Queue reorder spring(520, .83). A confirmation toast leaves after 2.6s; a provider's message stays until dismissed.
- *Daily details* — the moments repeated a hundred times a day, each in one of the three materials:
  - *Posters* (paper, `Posters`): a cover that stands alone (the stage, the wide Home hero) changes like a poster on a street. The old poster's tape gives at one top corner: it swings down from the other like a pendulum (up to 24°, past and back) and drops off the wall (680ms). 150ms after the tape gives, the next song's cover is slapped on over it — solid within a frame, from 120% and up to 5° (which way is the song's own) to flat on the kinetic curve (600ms) — and its tape goes on after it lands. Going back to an earlier song of the same queue peels the top poster up and away (7°, a tenth of its height, growing to 106%, fading, 260ms) and the earlier one is underneath, still taped. A cover goes up only once its picture has arrived (900ms at most), never a grey sheet filled in after; a song with the same cover (an album played through) keeps the poster up and only re-tapes it. One song is one poster: a song is never put up twice as it loads. "Your week" turns its pages with the gentler slap (112%, 3°).
  - *Repaint* (paint, `AmbientWash(spot)`): with the colour taken from the cover, a new song's colour is not faded into the room: it is poured out from the cover across the old one — a wavering round edge with seven runs reaching ahead, a band of the new colour's paint 3% ahead of the light it brings (1150ms, the enter curve). The room waits for the new cover to be measured (1.2s at most) so it never dips through the default family between songs; the app's own colour does the same.
  - *Lights down* (paint): while the song is paused, the stage's and Home's wash dims to 42% (900ms) and comes back up quicker when it plays (520ms), with the stage cover sitting back as before.
  - *The wait* (glass, `LiquidWait`): while a song starts, the play glyph is a drop going round a faint ring, not a stock spinner. Its head and tail are the droplet's two springs (900/.72, 240/.78) chasing a point that steps .36 of a turn every 400ms, so it stretches along the ring and gathers into a bead each step; the body is a little thinner than its head. Reduced motion: a short arc turning steadily.
  - *Running paint* (rails): seek and volume are painted, not slid. A jump (a tap further on, a song ending and the next starting at 0, a skip back) runs the paint to its new place on the run spring (320, .8: a small slosh, never a retreat you would read as a fault), a bead gathering at its head up to 90% wider while it runs; steady ticks and drags follow exactly. After a seek the rail and the times stay where they were put until the player reports that place (2.5s at most): they used to flick back first.
  - *The scrub drop* (paint, the stage): dragging the stage's rail raises a drop of the paint above the finger with the time in it (Inter 15 tabular, on-primary): it blooms out of the rail's head on the bloom spring, its body follows the finger on the droplet's slow spring while its neck stays on the paint's head — so it leans and stretches as it is dragged — and when let go it falls back into the rail.
  - *Where a click lands* (rails, desktop): a pointer resting on a rail lights the track up to it (the 10% fill, under the paint), and after resting 140ms the seek times read where a click would land, in full white, easing back to 62% when it leaves. Crossing the rail on the way to something else changes nothing.
  - *Skips kick* (`SkipTap`): next and previous push 6dp along their arrow, 10% narrower, and spring back on the press's overshoot spring; pressed again quickly, the kick starts over from where it is.
  - *Into the queue* (glass, `Flights`): a song added with a row's + leaves its row as a drop: its cover is tossed in an arc 120dp above the straight line, swelling to 135% toward its top, and drawn into the queue's door — the dock's queue glyph, or the phone's mini player — shrinking to a 12dp bead and rounding as it goes (640ms). The door swallows it as it lands: the dock's glyph swells 32% and rings out on the jelly spring in the one colour, the mini player gulps. Added any other way (the song menu), the glyph swallows at once. The + pops into a tick on the press's spring.
  - *Poured notices* (glass): the notice is born as a drop its own height across, falls into place on the bloom spring while spreading to both ends on the quick droplet spring, and its words come in after 35%; it leaves by pulling back into the drop. A confirmation's tick is drawn on, short leg then long (360ms, 140ms after).
  - *The pulled capsule* (glass, phone): the mini player can be pulled sideways. Its glass draws out from its far end like a drop pulled by a finger (up to 7% longer, half that thinner) while the song slides with the finger inside it, thinning; a way the queue cannot go hardly gives. Let go past a quarter of its width (or flicked at 900dp/s), it skips — left to the next song, right to the one before — and the new song slides in from the other side on the liquid spring as the glass gulps it; short of that, it rings back on the jelly spring.
  - *A held row* (paper): a queue row held by its handle comes off the wall like a strip of paper — 2.5% bigger, turned .9°, the raised fill under it — and is slapped back flat on the kinetic curve when let go.
- Reduced motion: 120ms fades, 160ms tweens, no pour, no rise, no kinetic letters, no drift, no travel; the tag and marks are drawn complete. Posters change with the picture's own cross-fade, the wash eases to a new colour, rails follow exactly, nothing kicks, flies or swallows, the notice fades.

**Interaction** — One pressable (`Modifier.tap`): no ripple, a squeeze-and-spring press; hover and keyboard focus lift the fill to 6% white, press to 10%. Rows let that fill bleed 8dp past the text column so covers and titles stay on the page edge. Rails (seek, volume) thicken under a pointer or a finger and have no thumb. Small controls draw at 34–36dp but answer across 44dp.

**Keys** (the desktop window, `Keys.kt`) — The keyboard plays the music from anywhere in the app: Space play or pause; ← → back or on 5 seconds (held, each step goes on from the last, and only the newest is sent once the one on its way has landed); Ctrl ← → previous or next song; Ctrl ↑ ↓ the volume, on the 5 % marks; M mute (and the volume back); L like; S shuffle; R repeat; Ctrl F or / search, with the caret in its field; N the stage; Q the queue; Esc closes what is on top (an artist page goes back one page); ? the keys page. The media keys work too. Letters are read by what they type, so they sit where the layout puts them; Alt and Super are left to the desktop. A text field with the caret keeps every key but Esc (which lets go of it) and the media keys, and a mouse press anywhere outside the field lets go of it, as in a browser. A held key repeats only for the steps. A key that changes something you might not be looking at says so in a notice (Shuffle on, Repeat one, Muted, Added to Liked songs); seeking, the volume and the places need no word. *The keys page*: a page of the sound notebook's ruled paper (one key to a line, the keys drawn as caps a step deeper at their lower edge), slapped onto the dimmed room on the kinetic curve (in 1.12× and turned 3°, coming to rest 1.2° off straight) and held by two strips of tape; ?, Esc or a click anywhere takes it down, lifting away.

**Song menu** — One menu for every song, opened by ⋯ (stage corner, dock, each row), a right click on the desktop or a long press on a phone (rows, shelf covers, the mini player). It is chrome, drawn by the shell above the pages (`SongMenuHost`), never inside the page that opened it.
- *Wide window*: a 344dp glass panel in the 28 card squircle. It is born as a drop the ⋯'s size at the button (or the pointer) and pours out on two springs — across on the quick droplet end (900, .72), down on the bloom (170, .7) — so it stretches into a bar before it fills; only its reveal is animated (a layer clip), never its size, so the glass keeps seeing what is behind it. The drop's rim light is drawn on the reveal's edge. Content fades in after 35% of the pour; closing pulls it back into the drop (200ms exit). It hangs below its anchor in the top half of the window and above it in the bottom half (the dock's ⋯ opens upward), keeps clear of the side panel and the dock, and centres on the anchor when neither side has 240dp. Escape or a click anywhere else closes it.
- *Phone*: an opaque sheet (the surface step; on a phone the stage is a sheet outside the wall, so glass there would blur the wrong page), top corners the 40 squircle, rising on the bloom spring over a 56% scrim. Its handle is liquid: it stretches with the sheet's speed and gathers back when it stops. Pulled down past a quarter, or flicked, it closes; back steps out of a sub-page first.
- *Content*: the song (52 cover, title, artist · service) with its heart; four tiles (18 squircle, 96dp, glyph top-left, name at the foot, up to two lines) — from a list: Play next, Add to queue, Start radio, Add to playlist; from the player: Start radio, Add to playlist, Share, Open on the service — then one group of rows: from the player, volume (phone), Sleep timer (its state and a chevron) and Keep screen on (Android); the rest of the actions; and, in red, Remove from this playlist / from the queue where it came from. On glass the tiles and the group are a 10% white veil; on the sheet, surface2.
- *Pages*: Add to playlist (New playlist first, its name typed into the row; then your own lists with a ring that becomes a drop with a tick that springs in) and Sleep timer (a ring of the primary that drains with the time left — or with the song, for "End of song" — beside a tabular countdown; options with a tick; Turn off in red). A page slides in from the side it leads to (a third of the width), the panel springs to its height on the liquid spring (420, .68) and the glass gulps it, with a band of light crossing it; rows rise in.
- *The heart* fills like a glass: the primary pours in from the bottom of the outline on the liquid spring, its surface sloshing on the jelly spring (300, .32) until still, and the heart gives a gulp; unliking drains it (280ms). On the stage it sits beside the song's words and keeps still while the words change.
- *Toggle*: a 52×32 pill; on, the primary with a drop of on-primary ink; off, the 10% fill with a 62% white drop. The drop's two ends move on the droplet springs, so it stretches across and gathers; pressed, it reaches a quarter knob toward the side it will go to.

**Update sheet** — A new release, shown by itself once it is ready to act on (downloaded, put in place, or out where Valen only tells), 1.2s after it becomes so; once per release a run. "Later" (or closing it any way) keeps it away a day for that release; a newer one shows at once. Settings → About's update row opens it again (`ui/update/UpdateSheet.kt`, `UpdateSheetHost`, beside the song menu).
- *Wide window*: a 440dp glass panel in the 28 card squircle, poured out of a drop in the middle of the content area on the song menu's two springs, over the sheets' 56% scrim (it came by itself). A new state sends a band of light across it and the glass gulps (the jelly spring); a download's ticks do not. *Phone*: the song menu's opaque sheet, sprung up on the bloom spring with the liquid handle, pulled down to close.
- *Head*: the tag, written in the primary as it opens (128dp); "A new Valen, {name}" in Bricolage 28/32 rising letter by letter; a two-tone lead-in — the new version in white semi-bold, "test build · you have 1.0.0-alpha.4" at 62%.
- *What changed*: a Bricolage 19 section title, then the release's own notes on one raised step (glass: the 10% veil; sheet: surface2) in the 18 squircle — GitHub's headings in Inter 15, its items behind the About page's 12dp pink dash, links reduced to "#2" and "@name", the "Full Changelog" line left out.
- *To be installed*: the file as a row (48 tile in the 12 squircle with the install glyph, the file's name, its size and state — the count while it downloads, tabular), the line rising in as the old one leaves upward. A copy Valen does not update says where it is updated instead.
- *The action*: one 52dp pill (Inter 15 semi-bold): Update / Download and install / Install / Restart now / Try again / Open release page. A download pours it full: the solid fill fades out (200ms) while the primary runs in from the left, the label white over the track and on-primary over the paint, cut at the paint's edge; at the end the paint runs the last of the way, the pill gives on the squish spring and the next word rises in. Under it, Release page and Later as tonal pills side by side. The actions stay put; the head and notes scroll above them and thin out over 24dp at an edge they pass.
- Everything rises in 38ms apart as it opens; reduced motion fades it all and draws the tag complete.

**Sound notebook** — `Route.Sound` (`ui/sound/`), opened from the stage's equalizer button (in the one colour while effects are doing something) and from Sound in the player's menu. A wide window shows a notebook open on the wall: two pages of the surface step joined by a spiral binding (rings through punched holes, in 62% white with a 100% glint); a phone stacks them under a binding along the top.
- *Left page — the song*: ruled (hairlines 28dp apart, the margin at 44dp in the primary at 32%), the date top right ("Sun 15 Nov"), the cover pasted over the margin and taped, the title (Inter 18), the artist, and its facts as a field note lists them ("on SoundCloud", "length", "from" the album, "played 8 times", "kept in Liked songs"). Under it, the **notes**: one line per effect, in the order they were taken, each two rules tall — the effect and its value in Inter 15 with the time it was written (tabular, 38%), the few words under it (62%) — with a dab of the marker in the margin as its bullet. The first line is the page's heading in Bricolage 19 (the preset, "My sound", "As recorded", "Effects off"). While a Spotify song plays a line says it keeps its own sound.
- *Writing*: a note is written at a pen's pace (26ms a letter, 900ms at most), revealed left to right with a nib — a dot of the marker — riding the edge; its words fade in after. A value that changes is struck out with the marker (260ms, a slightly uphill line through the letters) and the new one written in its place; an effect turned off is struck through and folds away. A setting is written only once it has held for 420ms, so a dragged slider is one note. Opening the notebook writes what is set line after line, 170ms apart.
- *Right page — the settings*: a dot grid (16dp, 11% white), behind index tabs (Presets, EQ, Time, Space, Tone; the open one is the page's own paper and stands up, the rest sit back and lower; on a phone they share the width). Changing tab **turns the page** over the binding: forward, the open page lifts at its free edge and swings past edge-on (−105°) shading as it rises, uncovering the next; back, the earlier page swings in over it (640ms, the enter curve). Under reduced motion they cross-fade.
- *Presets* are stickers: a 10% white label in the 18 squircle, a little crooked (±1.4°), glyph, name, three words; the chosen one gets a loop of the marker drawn round it (720ms, a hand-drawn circle whose ends cross).
- *EQ* is a sketch: the response really applied (RBJ biquads: ten peaking bands Q 1.41, shelves at 100 Hz and 8 kHz, Butterworth cuts, the preamp) as one 3dp line of the primary, shaded at 13% to the 0 dB line, over pencil guides at ±6 and ±12 with their numbers in the margin and the band frequencies under it; a dot on each band (12dp, 18 held, with its value in a pill above). The line pours into a new shape on the liquid spring and is drawn on by pen the first time.
- *Time* is two **rulers**: a tape that slides under a fixed needle of the marker, ticks every step, taller every five, labelled at the quarters (speed) or every two semitones (pitch), fading out at both edges; drag it or fling it — it runs out and settles on a tick, catching at 1× / 0. The value stands above it big and tabular with a word ("slower", "deeper", "follows the speed").
- *Space* and *Tone* are **pencil sliders**: the name and its value (in the primary once it moves off rest), a pencil line with quarter ticks, the marker's ink from where it rests to where it is (from the middle for ±), a notch at rest, and a dot of the marker to hold (14dp, 22 held). A tap puts it there, a drag moves it, a double tap returns it to rest, and near rest it catches. Frequencies travel by octaves; Muffle runs the other way (more muffled to the right); "Off" is always the far left.
- The *Effects* switch stands in the title row; any change switches effects on.

**Lyrics** — The song's words on the stage (`ui/lyrics/`), opened by the lyrics button beside Sound (in the one colour while shown; the choice is kept in `AppSettings.lyrics`). A wide window: the cover sits back to 80% on the lean spring and the column beside it widens to 680dp — the song's line (title in Inter 24, artist), the words filling the height, the rail and the transport (play 60) under them; volume, the sleep pill and Up next step aside. A phone: the words stand where the cover was, under a line with the cover (56, the 12 squircle), the title, the artist and the heart. The two layouts swap with the old leaving first (200ms exit, sinking) and the new rising in once it has mostly gone (320ms enter, delayed 150ms); the pane fades between looking, found and none.
- *Type*: Inter SemiBold, never Bricolage — 34/42 (−0.035em) on a wide stage, 26/32 on a phone (`LyricType`); backing vocals under their line in Inter Medium 21/26 (17/22), dimmer. A second voice is set to the right.
- *The lines*: the line being sung stands at 30% of the pane's height (24% on a phone), forward at full size while the rest sit back to 96.5%; to come and over are white at 40%, backing vocals at 30%. The pane's words thin out over its top 10% and bottom 20% (a mask, not a gradient on the page). The lines follow the song on a soft spring (150, .84), and while they travel each line below the leading one lags 7.5% more of what is left to go (up to seven lines), the ones above go a little ahead — they spread like poured paint and gather where they stop. A scroll by hand looks around; 2.6s after the last touch the words find the song again. A tap on a line plays from it (the press lies under the words, 18 squircle, hover 6%). The rows rise in 38ms apart, nearest the song first, when the words arrive.
- *Singing*: a word-timed line is one paragraph drawn in white and masked — sung is full white, unsung 40%, the edge between them soft (half an em) and moving through each word at the pace it is sung. A word lifts 0.07em as its singing starts (360ms, enter curve) and stays up until the line is over; a note held 1s or more has a crest run through its letters as it is held. A line timed as a whole comes up and lifts as one. The time is carried forward on the display's frames between the coordinator's reports and eased onto each (a third at a time; a jump over 400ms is taken as it is).
- *The pen*: on word-timed lyrics only (the pen follows the voice), the round marker of the tag goes over about one sung word in six — never two side by side, at most two to a line, the same way every time for the same song, the same kind never twice running. A word sung again in its line is crossed out (strike, double strike, X, scratched out, hatched); a held note is circled or underlined *while it is held*, the underline's paint running further the longer the note; a shouted word gets a burst of three dashes off its top corner, a loop or a box; long words get any of them, a zigzag under, or a swipe of the container tone behind the letters. Destructive marks start 70ms after the word ends and are drawn at a pen's pace (260 / 400 / 560ms, enter curve per stroke, the pen lifting between strokes), with a fine overspray where the pen first touched; everything is sized in ems so a phone's mark is the same mark smaller. Marks are drawn by time, not by event: seeking back unwrites them. On the line being sung they are the full primary; on lines past, 72%.
- *Pauses*: a gap of 4.5s or more opens a row of three dabs of the primary that fill one after another across it (rising on the back-out curve) and swell and go as the next line comes.
- *States*: while the words are looked up, flat blocks in the skeleton tone stand where the lines will be (no shimmer); untimed words read as a page in Inter 22/30 under "These words are not timed to the song."; none — "No lyrics for this song" / "None of the lyrics sources has its words."; an instrumental — "No lyrics" / "This one is an instrumental."; a failure — "Lyrics did not load", the source's own words, and Try again. The credit ("Lyrics from Lyprio · Musixmatch") ends the words in 38% white.
- *Reduced motion*: the lines move on a 160ms tween with no cascade, words switch whole at their start with no lift or wave, marks appear drawn, the clock follows the reports (no frames).
- *Settings → Lyrics*: one group of three switches (the app's `Toggle`, the whole row is the press) under a line saying where the words come from: Show on the stage, Pen marks (off: no word is marked), Moving words (off: words still fill as they are sung, but nothing lifts or ripples, the lines move together and the sung line keeps the others' size).

**This device, downloads, Discord** — No new pieces: a device list is a `PlaylistRow`/`PlaylistCover` like any other, its source glyph is Material's `FolderOpen` (no logo), and "From this device" on Import is a `SectionHeader`, one sentence and two `QuietButton`s (Choose a playlist, Choose a folder) above the lists found in ~/Music. A playlist's download is one `QuietButton` beside Play/Shuffle/Refresh that says its state — Download → Downloading → Downloaded (Material's download glyphs) — and, while it runs, the import's own pattern under the buttons: a count sentence ("Downloading 4 of 40"), the 4dp `ProgressRail`, the song on its way in `Text3`; a service asking Valen to slow down replaces the count with that sentence. Done, it leaves one quiet line: where the songs are. Remove download asks once in place, as Remove does. Settings → Downloads is a `Group` of `SwitchRow`s with one sentence of state above.
- *Settings → Discord* is the card in every part: one sentence of state above, "Show on Discord", then, with it on, the card as the profile carries it — a 56dp cover (or Valen's mark) in the 12 squircle with the mark as a 20dp badge ringed in `Text3` on its corner, the member list's line ("Listening to Valen") and the two lines beside it, the bar, and the two buttons as pills under it — then a title, a sentence and a raised surface per part: In the member list (`SegmentedFilter`: Valen, First line, Second line), The two lines (two field rows with their `LinkField`, a "Shows: ..." line that resolves the template for the playing song, and the words as `FilterPill`-shaped chips that add their token), The pictures (cover and mark as `SwitchRow`s), On hover (free text for the cover and for the mark), The time (`SegmentedFilter`: Bar, Count up, Count down, Off), While paused (Keep it, 5 min, 15 min, Hide now) and Buttons (the song's page; Valen's page). The preview is only there with a song on show; the words are a template filled from the song ({title}, {artist}, {album}, {source}, {queue}, {line}, {status} and {line|artist} fallbacks), so nothing on the card is a fixed string. The Discord card itself is outside the app and follows the same words rule; its buttons are only drawn for other people.

**Your week** — A story of the last seven days (`ui/recap/`), told from what Valen counted (`core/Recap.kt`), opened from Settings → Your week ("Watch your week") and from a card on Home and in the profile that shows by itself at the end of each week (Sunday and Monday, `recapDue`), or always with Settings → Test → "Your week on Home" (its three covers stacked like flyers, the time, the artist on top, Watch). It is poured over the whole app like Home's page (the pour in the primary) and leaves with a 200ms fade.
- *A poster*: designed at 400dp wide and scaled as a whole (a density of its own), so a page is laid out once — the full screen on a phone (its height free, insets kept), a 9:16 poster as tall as the window allows on a wide window, in the 40 squircle on the black wall with bare chevrons beside it. Each page's ground is the wall with its song's wash drifting (fade .55) and the grain over it: one wash on screen.
- *Chrome*: a 3dp segment per page (28% white; read ones full; the shown one filling), "Your week" and its days (hidden on the first page, which says it big), Pause and Close; at the foot, a pill of the page's song with the playing bars. A hold stops the page and, after 220ms, hides the chrome; the poster gives to 98.5% on the squish spring.
- *Turning*: a tap on the right two thirds or → turns on, the left third or ← goes back, Space pauses, Escape (or back on a phone) closes. A page that comes next is slapped on as paper — solid within a frame, from 112% and ±3° to flat on the kinetic curve, the old page sinking to 96.5% and 35% under it; going back lifts the top page away (fade and grow to 106%, 200ms) over the one before.
- *Pages* (each left out when it has nothing to say): the opening (the tag written, "Your week" in Bricolage 66/62 with a marker under it, the days, the three top covers fanned and slapped up, the middle one taped); the time (counted up in Bricolage 72/70 in the primary, a sentence against the week before or per day, and the seven days poured down from a marker line as columns of paint with drips, the busiest in the primary with its time under it); the artist (their most played cover pasted and taped, the name in Inter 52 with a marker under it, time and plays); the top artists and the top songs (ranked rows rising 110ms apart, bars growing, the pen looping the first rank, the song that plays swiped like the playing row); new to you (the count, the three most played new songs fanned); when you listen (a 24-hour dial of paint, midnight at the top, every hour's track faint and its time poured out along it sweeping round, the pen looping the busiest hour's end, the hour big in the middle); the song of the week (its cover in the 40 squircle pasted and taped, its name in the stage's type with a marker under it, the pen round its plays); the end ("That was your week", the five top covers pasted over each other, Play these and Done). Pages stay 6.5s, 8s or 9.5s (a list, the song of the week); the end waits.
- *Sound*: each page plays a song it is about, from about a third of the way in, 320ms after it lands (the first after the pour), never the same song twice while another is left; a page sharing the last one's song lets it play on. The player is lent (`PlaybackCoordinator.lend`): nothing played counts, and what was playing comes back where it was, playing if it was. Play these makes the week's ten songs the queue.
- *Reduced motion*: pages cross-fade, nothing slaps, rises or counts, the pen is drawn complete, the wash is still.

**Words** — Short and plain, everywhere in the app: say what a thing is or does in as few common words as it takes ("Pick artists you love", "Nothing found. Try more sources."). No jargon, no clever lines. The one emoji in the app is 🥺 in the welcome's "Hi there 🥺", a wink the owner asked for; nothing else uses emoji.

**Layout** — Screens draw edge to edge; `LocalChrome` tells them what the chrome takes, and `ContentColumn` keeps content clear of it and inside the gutters at the full width the window gives — a wide window is room for more music, not more margin. Only a screen that is read rather than browsed passes `maxWidth` (Settings and Import: a 720dp reading column, centred). Grids earn a column for every ~300–350dp (Up next: 1–6 across), and the two big artworks grow with the window (hero cover 248/312/360, stage cover up to 720) so they are never a stamp in the corner of a wide band. ≥1000dp: 236dp floating side panel + 72dp floating dock (max 1000dp, centred under the content; gives up volume, then times, then the rail as it narrows). 700–999: the panel becomes a 68dp icon rail. <700: 60dp tab bar capsule (Home, Search, Playlists, Now Playing; the current one spells its name) + 64dp mini player capsule; Now Playing, the queue and Import are full-screen sheets. Navigation state lives above the width switch, so resizing across a breakpoint keeps your place. Safe areas respected; desktop min 900×640; phone reference 390×844.

**Rules** — One accent and at most one wash per screen. Hit targets ≥44dp. Rows truncate at one line, headlines at two. Material Symbols Rounded only; no service logos. Empty states are a title and a sentence about real state on the page's left edge, with at most one action (the stage alone centres its text). Provider failures are shown in the provider's words and never time out.
